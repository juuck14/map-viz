Map coloring, step by step

Four colors are always enough to color a map so that neighboring regions differ. Knowing that does not tell you which color to choose next. Color a map yourself, then watch three strategies make their choices, get stuck, and recover.

Pick a color, then click regions. Click a region again with the same color to clear it.

    The rules

    Each region is one connected piece. Two regions are neighbors when they share a stretch of border; touching at a single point does not count. A coloring is valid when no two neighbors have the same color. The Four Color Theorem (Appel and Haken, 1976) says every such map can be colored with four colors. It does not say how to find the coloring.

    Map and graph

    Put a dot in every region and join two dots when the regions are neighbors. Coloring the map is the same as coloring the dots so that joined dots differ. Each line of the graph crosses the border it stands for.

    Checking

    Neighbors are found from the shapes themselves: two borders must overlap along a segment. A separate checker reads neighbors directly from the grid each map is drawn on, and re-checks every finished coloring shown here.
