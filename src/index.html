Map coloring, step by step

Four colors are always enough to color a map so that neighboring regions differ. Knowing that does not tell you which color to choose next. Color a map yourself, then watch seven strategies make their choices, get stuck, and recover.

Under the hood

Pick a color, then click regions. Click a region again with the same color to clear it.

    The rules

    Each region is one connected piece. Two regions are neighbors when they share a stretch of border; touching at a single point does not count. A coloring is valid when no two neighbors have the same color. The Four Color Theorem (Appel and Haken, 1976) says every such map can be colored with four colors. It does not say how to find the coloring.

    Map and graph

    Put a dot in every region and join two dots when the regions are neighbors. Coloring the map is the same as coloring the dots so that joined dots differ. Each line of the graph crosses the border it stands for.

    The maps

    The maps are generated: random points are turned into Voronoi cells, noise decides which cells are land, and regions grow from seeds across the land. Shared borders are stored once, so both neighbors use exactly the same line.

    Checking

    The page finds neighbors from the drawn shapes: two borders must overlap along a segment. A separate checker uses the neighbor list the generator recorded from the Voronoi cells, and re-checks every finished coloring shown here.
